Ocean warming is anticipated to alter marine community composition and ecological interactions. In a 4.5-month mesocosm experiment, we examined how experimental warming and transient upwelling affected the biomass and ecological network properties of coastal benthic communities.

Extreme warming reduced total community biomass by approximately 50%. The biomass of the foundation species *Fucus* spp. declined by 50–76%, with complete loss at +5°C. This decline was associated with a reduction of approximately 39% in non-trophic interactions. Extreme warming also caused the disappearance of predatory sea stars and reduced network connectance, omnivory, generality, and carnivorous energy fluxes.
